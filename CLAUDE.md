## How it works
- **Router** (`wbc/agent/loop.py`): Muse Spark 1.3 on the Responses API at `effort: low`, with parallel tool calls. Every call sends the whole conversation, about 2 s a call. Before Spark's first call of a turn, code fetches `pins.h`, the schematic and the board read (`router.prefetch`). The board read and the schematic are made once, at start-up, and kept for the session, checked against each turn's `pins.h`, until Spark calls `read_breadboard` itself. A question no tool is needed for ("what should this read when I press?") goes to Muse Spark Open on Baseten (`open`, with `BASETEN_API_KEY`) with the bench facts and no tools; requests, "why", a failure, and questions only the cameras, the arm, the notebook, the spend or the conversation can answer (`OPEN_ELSEWHERE`) go to Spark. Spark Open replies PASS when the facts don't answer it, and its client gives up after 20 s with no retry (`open.timeout_s`); either way Spark takes the turn. `--backup` is a Chat Completions router for an OpenAI-compatible server; it has never run live.
- **Coder** (`wbc/agent/coder.py`): `ask_coder` sends a task, the bench facts and up to 4 files to Muse Wonder. A diff has to `git apply --check` and compile in the sandbox, then `auto_stage` applies it to the working tree. `build_and_flash` uploads it once you say go. `read_sensor` (`wbc/agent/dut.py`) is the force sensor board's: it reads that board's serial port, `mv <reading>` every 20 ms and `# reading GPIO <n>` every 50 readings, without resetting the board.
- **Add a part** (`wbc/agent/parts.py`): `add_part` has Muse Spark Open work out how to wire a part from `parts.yaml` (the SSD1306 screen) to the DevKit's free GPIOs and drive it. Code checks the wiring (every pin once, power to power, a free GPIO that can drive an output and isn't a boot or serial pin) and sends it back once with what's wrong. The answer is saved as a skill in `.wbc/skills/`, and the part is drawn into a copy of the schematic in `.wbc/schematics/`, kept only if KiCad's rules check passes and its netlist joins every pin as wired; with `--show` KiCad's window shows the copy. Nothing on the board changes.
- **Arm fast path** (`wbc/agent/fast_arm.py`): plain rules find an arm command, its kind from trigger words, its thing and place from `fast_arm` in `models.yaml` or a note's quoted place. Llama 3.2 3B on `mlx_lm.server` (port 8090) has to name the same kind, and then the tool runs with no Spark call. Questions, negations, "I" and "we" statements, a bare "that" and unknown things go to Spark, and so does a move that fails, to explain it. Without the server the fast path is off.
- **Memory** (`wbc/agent/memory.py`): each run records its turns in `.wbc/session.json`; `--resume` picks up the last session, and `--sim` keeps its own. A conversation becomes a summary at 60,000 input tokens, 40 turns or two failed turns in a row. After a tool has run, a summary asks what's still to do rather than run it again. Turns a summary folds away stay in the session (`earlier`, the newest 400), and the ones a request's words point to come back whole in that turn's message (`for_turn`), not the instructions, which stay the same turn to turn: one turn either side within 5 minutes, at most 3 matches and 3,000 characters. Scoring (`ranked`): BM25 (k1 1.2, b 0.75) over whole words with the common ones left out (`terms`), times (1 + 1 / ln(e + age)) / 2, times `importance` (1.5 for a turn whose tools changed the bench, 0.25 more for a failed tool). Every call's instructions carry the newest 20 notes; up to 5 older ones the request points to go into its message. `memory.embeddings` (an OpenAI-compatible embeddings server, unset) adds matches by meaning through reciprocal rank fusion; it has never run against a real server. Spark Open gets the last 6 turns and the same recalled context. Without `--resume` a run starts with no earlier turns, so a take never sees a rehearsal's.
- **Voice** (`wbc/agent/ears.py`, `mouth.py`): the Razer (`ears.input_device`) stays open, and a loudness gate cuts it into utterances (30 ms frames, 12 dB over the room's noise, 0.8 s of quiet ends one). Every utterance is transcribed, and only those starting with "Copilot" go to the router. "Copilot, stop", or "Stop!" alone, goes straight to `Bench.halt()`, even mid-turn and while the co-pilot speaks. After a reply that asks something, an answer of up to five words needs no "Copilot," for 8 s. Replies are said by Kokoro-82M on this Mac (`mouth.command: scripts/kokoro-say`, its server on :8767), the first two sentences, with the mic muted while it speaks; without the stand-in the voice is off, never macOS `say`.
- **HUD** (`hud/`, `wbc/agent/web_hud.py`): `--hud` serves the orb page at http://127.0.0.1:8766/ and drives it over Server-Sent Events, standard library only: listening, the request, every model call under its own name with its time (transcriptions only for a request), the reply's first sentences (144 characters at most) and its speech, the turn's time and spend. Not yet: the board view, the netlist and the diff.
- **Board reader** (`wbc/perception/`): the carrier's tag36h11 ids 10–13 at 20 mm map the photo to the sheet; SAM 3.1 segments "wire" in a 5.5 px/mm top-down view; the hole grid fit shifts, turns and scales each board within 1.2 mm, 1° and 0.97–1.06; wire ends come from thinning, moved 1 mm past each mask's tip (`tip_inset_mm` −1.0) for the bare leg, and snap to holes within 1.3 mm; the netlist is checked against `pins.h`. `read_schematic` reads the board's KiCad schematic (`bench.yaml`'s `schematic`) at start-up too: its nets and the electrical rules check, through `kicad-cli`. Flat jumpers only. The camera keeps the sharpest frame from 1.5–3 s after opening.
- **Desk camera** (`wbc/perception/desk.py`, `config/desk.yaml`): a C920 71 cm over the desk. Tags 20–25 (50 mm) and the carrier's 10–13 (20 mm, `carrier_ids`, counted only beside 2 of the desk's own), touched with the arm, map it into the arm's frame (`.wbc/desk_tags.json`), and `locate` gives a thing's middle, size, heading and grip (1 cm from its middle toward its narrow end: a screwdriver's handle, toward the tip), corrected for its height. With `--arm` the agent keeps the desk camera open, so a photo doesn't wait for it to settle.
- **Arm driver** (`wbc/motion/arm.py`): the one owner of the bus. `Arm.open()` never calls `robot.connect()` and refuses servos that don't match the calibration file. `power_on()` brings the joints up one at a time from the base out. Moves peak at 45°/s and 180 mm/s, with no goal over 5° from its joint; torque caps 50%, the gripper's too (its overload threshold goes from LeRobot's 25% to 60% while powered, and back at `close()`); P 32 on every joint but the wrist roll, written after LeRobot's setup at every power-on. Every motor is cut on a 3° jump at torque-on, a 5° stray, a 5 mm sag under a floor or a bus failure, and a cut latches until `clear_stop()`. `halt()` holds the arm with its motors on. `move_to()` re-aims each joint to within 0.2°, and on the way down to a grip keeps the tips over `GRIP_FLOOR`, about 3 mm over the desk, instead of the tool floor. `grip()` reads empty when the jaws stop under `EMPTY` 22 ticks from closed.
- **Arm skills** (`wbc/motion/hand.py`, `table.py`; tools `locate`, `pick_up`, `put_down`, `move_thing`, `arm_home`): every pose is solved before the first move, and `move_thing` plans the pick and the put together, powering the arm on during the photo. The jaws close across a thing's long axis, with the fixed jaw beside it (`hand.moving_jaw: left`). Floors follow the desk as the arm's touches measured it: −7.4 mm at the pan axis, falling 92.0 mm per m of reach, plus 2 mm, and the jaw tips may come within 5 mm (`tool_floor_mm`). A pick opens the jaws only as wide as the thing needs and grips a quarter of the way up something lying flat. Jaws holding a handle read as shut as empty ones, so a pick that closed all the way comes back `unconfirmed`, and `move_thing` then looks once where the thing was. A put into a container in `hand.containers` (the mug, 98 mm) stands what's held in on end: the gripper turned up over the opening, level or 15–30° down, its narrow end first unless the wrist roll's stop rules that out, let go 15 mm inside the rim. Level reaches low enough from about 25–28 cm out. A refused arm move forgets where locate saw things, so asking again takes a new photo. With `--arm`, each arm request's moves go to `.wbc/logs/arm-<time>-<n>.json`. `hand.go_ahead: request` lets the request move the arm; `--enter` makes each move wait for Enter.

## Facts
- **Hardware:**
  - SO-101: 5 arm joints plus the gripper, STS3215 servos at ids 1–6 (gripper 6) on a 1 Mbps bus, through a CH343 adapter at `/dev/cu.usbmodem5B910297421`. The elbow (id 3) is a 1/345. Never power the servos from 12 V: the shoulder lift's limit is 8.0 V.
  - LeRobot's calibration file is `~/.cache/huggingface/lerobot/calibration/robots/so_follower/wbc_follower.json`. `lerobot-calibrate` writes each recorded range into the servos as position limits, and the driver won't open the arm unless the servos match the file. Switch the servo power off and on after changing ids or calibrating.
  - The jaws stop with about a 5 mm gap at the tips, and the multimeter probe can be gripped only there: at P 32 it stops them 24–25 ticks from closed, where closes on nothing stop at 19.
  - The wrist roll's limits keep it clear of ±180°, where its reading wraps. It also stops at about −80°, so `limits_deg` in `robot.yaml` keeps moves inside −78°. It can drive itself away as its torque comes on, which cuts every motor.
  - Single voltage and temperature readings glitch on the wrist and gripper servos. Take a median of about 5 before acting on either.
  - Two Logitech C920s: the board camera (index 1 in `bench.yaml`) about 24 cm over the breadboard, and the desk camera (index 0). Razer Seiren Mini mic. Three ESP32 DevKits; the demo board is a 30-pin DOIT DevKit, its pinout in `parts.yaml` unchecked against the silkscreen. Usdrwam TFS-100 force sensors (8 mm sensing area, 20 g–2 kg).
- **Demo board** (`pins.h`): `LED_PIN 5`, wired to GPIO 4 on purpose. The jumper from a5 (GPIO 4) to a26, 330 Ω from b26 to b42, the LED's anode in row 42 and its cathode in row 43, jumpered to a rail. The force sensor board (`hardware/demo_sensor.kicad_sch`, `read_sensor`) has a TFS-100 from the 3.3 V rail to row 26 and 10 kΩ from row 26 to ground, on the same jumper. `firmware.port: auto` picks the one USB serial port that isn't the arm's.
- **Joint map:** q = sign · (raw − 2047) · 2π / 4096, every sign +1. The URDF's zero is lerobot-calibrate's L pose. LeRobot's degrees put 0° at the middle of each calibrated range instead.
- **IK:** the tool frame is turned −90° about y so the approach axis is x, and the tool point is the closed jaws' tips. Reach is about 460 mm at table height. Poses behind the base need `q0`.
- **Meta Model API** (`https://api.meta.ai/v1`, OpenAI SDK):
  - Router `muse-spark-1.3`, coder `muse-wonder-1.0` (accepts `reasoning={"effort": "low"}`).
  - Voice: POST `/v1/asr/transcribe` as multipart, with a JSON `request` part (`model`, `audioEncoding: "WAV"`, `keywords`) and an `audio` part. It returns `{"transcript": ...}`. Keyword hints work best as acronyms.
  - SAM 3.1 (`sam-3.1`, https://dev.meta.ai/docs/sam/segmenting): POST `/v1/responses` with one `input_text` noun phrase and one `input_image` (data URL), `metadata` values as strings (`mask_encoding: one_bit`, `include_confidence: "true"`). One concept per request; no match is an empty reply. Each record is an id, a box (x2 and y2 inclusive) and a mask covering only that box. $2.50 per 1,000 images.
- **Muse Spark Open** on Baseten: `meta-models/muse-spark-open-1026`, OpenAI-compatible Chat Completions at `https://inference.baseten.co/v1`, with `BASETEN_API_KEY`.
- **Servos:** `wbc/motion/sts.py` talks to one servo directly for the read-only tools. Registers: Torque_Enable 40, Goal_Position 42, Torque_Limit 48, feedback block 56–70. The units of Present_Current (69) are unconfirmed.

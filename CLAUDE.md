- `squeeze` on a half breadboard (Oct 28, `.wbc/logs/gripper-20261028-110227.csv`). The jaws stopped at 1315, 37 ticks from closed. The servo held a steady load of 144 (14%) for 30 s, about 3.9 per mille per tick at P = 16, so it never reached the 20% cap.
  - A pull and a twist changed nothing: load and position stayed exactly put, and current moved 3–4× its noise. The servo's load is its own drive, P times how far the jaw is from its goal, so it moves only when the jaw does.
  - The one change came 3 s into the twist. The board shifted to a thinner spot, and the jaw closed 4 ticks in 30 ms (load 144 → 128).
  - So the gripper servo can't feel a pull or a slip along its jaws, only the gap changing. Slip detection needs the force-sensor pads, and weighing needs the arm's joints (the gravity model).
  - At `--gain 32 --torque-limit 240` (`gripper-20261028-111132.csv`), the board, gripped face to back, stopped the jaws at 1299, 21 ticks from closed. The load held at 160 (16%). P 32 doubled the push per tick (7.6 per mille), but there were fewer ticks to push with, so it still didn't reach the cap. The pull and twist again changed nothing.
  - Why so few ticks: a half breadboard (8.5–10 mm) is only a few mm thicker than the jaws' 5 mm gap at their stop, so the goal gets only 20–40 ticks past the board (37 in the first run, gripped deeper). For the cap to set the squeeze, P has to be about 48–64 (`--gain` goes to 64). The other route is a force mode, such as the servo's PWM mode (LeRobot's `OperatingMode.PWM`), which drives at a set output wherever the jaw is. It's untested here.
  - `--gain` writes the gripper's P (EEPROM) before torque comes on and puts it back afterwards; on the real servo it read back 16, locked. 24% stays under the 25% overload start. Gripping deeper in the jaws also helps: the jaw stops further from closed, and the same torque presses harder nearer the pivot.

## Facts
- **Hardware:**
  - SO-101: 5 arm joints plus the gripper, servo IDs 1–6, gripper = 6. STS3215 servos on a 1 Mbps bus.
  - Three classic ESP32 DevKits: the demo board, the FSR sampler (ADC1 pins, GPIO 32–39), and a spare. An Arduino Uno spare.
  - Logitech C920 (1080p), Razer Seiren Mini mic, UMLIFE SSD1306 0.96" 7-pin SPI OLEDs.
  - Usdrwam TFS-100 thin-film force sensors: 10 mm head, 8 mm sensing area, 20 g–2 kg, two pins at 2.54 mm.
- **Demo board pins** (`pins.h`): SCK 18, MOSI 23, CS 5 (wired to 13 on purpose), DC 16, RES 17.
- **Meta Model API** (`https://api.meta.ai/v1`, OpenAI SDK):
  - Router `muse-spark-1.3` and coder `muse-wonder-1.0` (accepts `reasoning={"effort": "low"}`).
  - Voice: POST `/v1/asr/transcribe` as multipart, with a JSON `request` part (`model`, `audioEncoding: "WAV"`, `keywords`) and an `audio` part. It returns `{"transcript": ...}`. Keyword hints work best as acronyms only.
  - SAM 3.1 (`sam-3.1`), documented at https://dev.meta.ai/docs/sam/segmenting and built in `wbc/perception/sam_client.py`: POST `/v1/responses` with one `input_text` noun phrase and one `input_image` (data URL), `metadata` values as strings (`mask_encoding: one_bit`, `include_confidence: "true"`). One concept per request; no match is an empty reply, not an error. Each record is an id, a box (x2 and y2 inclusive) and a mask covering only that box; the parser gives half-open bounds. $2.50 per 1,000 images (Meta's pricing page), 3,000 requests a minute. The `facebook/sam3.1` weights (and `facebook/sam3`) are gated, and access is still pending (Oct 28).
- **Force sensor wiring:** each sensor runs from 3V3 to its pin, with 10 kΩ from the pin to GND. SD, SP, MD, MP = GPIO 32, 33, 34, 35. The ESP32 reads nothing below about 142 mV, so an unloaded sensor reads that floor. The sampler is a CP2102 board (`10C4:EA60`). The arm's servo adapter is a CH343 (`1A86:55D3`) at `/dev/cu.usbmodem5B910297421`.
- **IK:** the tool frame is turned −90° about y so the approach axis is x. Yaw may move up to ±20° from the target's bearing (`IKResult.yaw`). Reach is about 460 mm at table height. Poses behind the base need `q0`.
- **Servos:** LeRobot's bus API moved (`FeetechMotorsBus` is deprecated in favor of `SerialMotorsBus`), so `wbc/motion/sts.py` talks to one servo directly. Registers: Torque_Enable 40, Goal_Position 42, Torque_Limit 48, feedback block 56–70. The units of Present_Current (69) are unconfirmed.
- **Joint map:** q = sign · (raw − 2047) · 2π / 4096, with sign +1 on all five arm joints (checked Oct 28). The URDF's zero is the calibration's L pose. LeRobot's degrees put 0° at the middle of each calibrated range, (raw − mid) · 360 / 4095: that's −11.9° off the URDF's zero for the base and under 5° for the rest. The arm goes past the URDF's limits (at rest the lift reads −111°), so moves should use each joint's calibrated range. The wrist roll is the exception: it has no stop, so the URDF's limits protect its cable.
